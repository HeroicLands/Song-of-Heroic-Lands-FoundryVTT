Jewelcraft is gem-cutting, goldsmithing and silversmithing, and the fine metalwork that surrounds both — a ring, an amulet, a torc, a brooch, a chain, and more rarely a diadem or a small figure. A finished piece usually weighs around **2d6 ounces**, up to triple that for something substantial and half for a ring or a pair of earrings. Cut stones are traded on their own account as well as set into work. Time varies enormously with ambition; **3d6 days** is typical.

A Jewelcraft Success Value test on the standard table fixes the quality of the piece.

**Price.** A finished piece is worth the weight of its base form times the price per ounce of its material — the jeweller's own outlay being a third of that. Most pieces are also set with **1d6** cut stones, each adding the **square of its weight in carats** times the stone's listed value. An eight-carat pearl is therefore worth 640d, which is why weight matters so much more than count.
