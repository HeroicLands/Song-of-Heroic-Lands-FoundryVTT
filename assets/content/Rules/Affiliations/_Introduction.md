An **affiliation** is a character's standing with some body of people: a
religion or one of its sects, an arcane school or convocation, a craft guild, a
criminal syndicate, a knightly order, a household, a war-band. It records not
what a character can do but who will vouch for them, and how far.

This is a different thing from a skill, and the two answer different questions.
A skill asks whether a character can perform an act; an affiliation asks whether
they are entitled to. A physician with no guild standing may set a bone as ably
as any master, but they cannot draw on the guild's teaching, its houses, or its
protection when a patient dies under their hands.

## What an affiliation records

Four things, of which only the last is a number:

- **Society** — the body itself: which temple, which guild, which convocation.
- **Office** — the position held within it, where the character holds one.
- **Title** — the form of address the office carries, where it carries one.
- **Level** — the depth of the character's standing, from nothing upward.

**Level** is the part that moves. It rises with service, patronage and the slow
accumulation of good standing, and it falls with negligence, scandal and
transgression. It can also be altered by effects that bear on standing directly:
a public disgrace or a granted dispensation adjusts the level rather than
replacing it, so the character's history remains legible underneath.

A character may hold as many affiliations as their life has earned, and they
need not sit comfortably together. Standing in two rival houses is a
complication worth playing, not an error.

## Affiliation as credential

Where an affiliation matters most is as the **source of authority** behind
mystical practice. A Mystery or a Mystical Ability may name the affiliation that
credentials it — the temple whose rites a priest is ordained to perform, the
convocation whose teaching an arcanist was admitted to. The power is the
character's, but the standing to wield it legitimately is the affiliation's, and
a character stripped of standing keeps the training while losing the right.

How far that goes is a matter for the body being described. Some treat their
teaching as a trade secret and count its unlicensed use as theft; others care
only that the rites are performed correctly, by whoever performs them.

## Using affiliations for particular bodies

The same four fields serve very different institutions. What changes between
them is what standing _buys_, and what it costs to lose.

**Craft and trade guilds.** Level is the apprentice-journeyman-master ladder,
and office is where a character sits in the guild's governance. Standing buys
the licence to practise openly, access to guild workshops and materials, the
right to take apprentices, and the guild's weight in a dispute. It rises with
accepted work and dues paid, and falls with shoddy work, undercutting, or
practising outside one's grade. Where a guild holds a monopoly, an unaffiliated
practitioner is not merely unsupported but unlawful.

**Religions and their sects.** Level is depth of ordination and standing in the
faith; office is the priestly rank. Standing gives the right to perform rites on
the congregation's behalf, access to temple resources, and a claim on the
hierarchy's protection. Record the religion as the affiliation and the
character's relationship with the god through [[Piety]] and [[Grace]] — the two
are related but not the same, and they can diverge sharply. A devout priest may
fall foul of their temple's politics without their god caring in the least, and
a heretic cast out by the hierarchy may retain every scrap of divine favour.
That gap is worth playing.

**Arcane schools and convocations.** Level is the grade of admission to the
body's teaching; office is a position within the school. Standing gates what a
character may be _taught_ and what texts they may consult, so it tends to govern
acquisition rather than use — an expelled arcanist keeps every ability already
learned and simply learns nothing further. Convocations are often jealous of
their material, and using it without standing may be treated as theft of the
work itself.

**Criminal syndicates.** Level is position in the organisation and office is the
role held within it, but standing behaves differently here: it buys protection,
fencing, safe houses, information and muscle, and it is paid for in obligation
rather than dues. Two things distinguish a syndicate from a guild. Standing is
usually **secret**, so it confers nothing a character is willing to invoke
publicly; and it is dangerous to lose — a guild expels, whereas a syndicate is
owed something, and collects. A character's standing may also be worth more to
their enemies as leverage than it is to them as an asset.

**Orders, households and war-bands.** Level is rank and office is command;
standing is held together by fealty and service rather than dues or contracts.
It buys maintenance, equipment, the right to command others, and a lord's
backing, and it is lost through dishonour or broken oath. Obligation here runs
in both directions, and a character can be as much held by their standing as
helped by it.

The pattern generalises. When adding a body of your own, decide three things:
what standing buys, what raises and lowers it, and what happens to a character
who loses it. A body where the answer to the third question is "nothing much" is
probably background colour rather than an affiliation.

## Where the affiliations are

**SoHL ships no affiliations of its own.** Religions, schools, convocations,
guilds and syndicates are all setting material: they belong to a particular
world, its history and its politics, and none of that is assumed here. The
system provides the mechanism — the way standing is recorded, the way it moves,
and the way a mystical practice points at the body that credentials it — and
leaves the bodies themselves to the setting.

Setting modules supply them, as they supply the skills, creatures and mystical
practices particular to their worlds. A campaign that wants guilds and temples
should install the module for its setting, or author its own affiliations
directly in the world.
