Armour expense tables are generated by the three SQL fences in [[doc-crafting#armour-expense|Crafting]], using the shared content index and normal journal/site table rendering. The source armour notes remain unchanged; the build writes neither materials nor hours into their frontmatter or item system data. These figures are journal reference data, not a new crafting runtime API.

The query reads `sohl.system.material`, `valueBase`, and `locations`. Flexible and rigid locations contribute their anatomical body fractions. A location named once in `locations.facing` contributes half; twice contributes its full fraction. This matches the coverage calculation checked in the armour content tests. Grade-specific price is already reflected in the authored `valueBase`; the derivation never substitutes a calculated sale price.

For the material's plain-grade rate and the craft's materials share `m` and ten-hour day rate `r`:

```text
plainPrice = coverage × baseRate
labour = (1 − m) × min(valueBase, plainPrice)
materials = valueBase − labour
hours = labour ÷ r × 10
```

The approved rates live beside the SQL query, in its `rates` relation, and are explained in the Crafting journal. Leather's plain grade is Rawhide; its rate is 400d per unit of coverage even when a luxury leather grade has a higher sale price. Luxury grades therefore increase materials without increasing labour beyond the plain-grade amount. Lower-price grades reduce both. No extra casting multiplier is applied by this armour derivation.

There is one generated table for each making craft: Textilecraft, Hideworking, and Weaponcraft. Rows link to their original armour articles and display sale price, materials to two decimal places in pence, and hours rounded to the nearest whole hour. Calculations use unrounded intermediate values. Display ties round upward; the SQL converts final display amounts to eight-place decimal values first to discard floating-point noise. Fast Crafting changes the time subsequently, not the materials.

The expense tests run these exact SQL fences against the authored armour inventory, verify complete coverage and craft assignment, and compare the output against the shared armour-coverage helper and independent expense arithmetic. This catches an omitted material or altered body fraction that would otherwise silently omit or misprice an article. Source notes are inputs only.
