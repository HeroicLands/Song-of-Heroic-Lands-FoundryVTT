A sliver of wood or bamboo tipped with a hardened point and wadded at the base with fluff or thistledown, cut to seal the blowgun's bore so a lungful of breath drives it the length of the tube rather than hissing past it. The dart alone delivers little force at the target end; it is carried and often poisoned rather than thrown with strength, so its work is done as much by what tips it as by how it flies. A blower keeps a tube of them dry and ready, since a damp wad seats poorly and ruins the seal.
