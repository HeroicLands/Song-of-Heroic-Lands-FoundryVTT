A creeping dread fills the air as the Helspawn emerge from the shadows — a host of ghastly figures summoned from the underworld by dark priests. Their appearances vary from grotesque and decayed to eerily beautiful, a testament to the fell magic that binds them. Some retain a semblance of their former selves but with a chill touch of death, while others are twisted and malformed, their bodies marred by necrotic energy. Each Helspawn exudes an unsettling aura, a tangible sense of the unnatural that makes one's skin crawl and breath catch in the throat.

When they move, it is with a graceless, relentless determination for some, and a terrifying, fluid elegance for others. Their hollow eyes, whether ablaze with malevolent intelligence or empty and vacant, speak of an unholy existence bound to serve their necromantic masters. The mere presence of Helspawn can dampen light, casting an oppressive gloom that chills the soul and foretells doom.

## Game Master Description

Helspawn are the undead — the dead made animate through the power of gods, celestial agents, or mortal priests who have been granted necromantic authority. They are found across all of Thalorna, wherever the right kind of divine power holds sway, and the phenomenon is not limited to any single pantheon. "Helspawn" is a generic term for "undead". Every major religious tradition that touches on death or chaos has its own theological explanation for why the dead sometimes walk, and every such tradition has those who can — or who dare to — call them back.

  

The cosmological basis for Helspawn is the soul-mechanics established in [[Souls_and_Spirits]]. Every Helspawn is, at root, a soul that has been pulled back from the spirit realm (or kept from completing its transit) and bound to a corporeal form on the material plane. The work always involves coercion or arrangement: souls do not naturally return to their bodies. Something — a necromancer, a ritual, a creator-Helspawn, a divine agent — must act to make the binding happen.

  

Crucially, **no mortal can make themselves Helspawn**. The transformation requires action by an external agent on a soul that has either died or is in the process of dying. A would-be Nightwight cannot retrieve their own soul once they have died; they cannot perform the binding ritual on themselves while dying; they cannot self-apply the transformation through any combination of preparation. Whatever role the candidate plays in the process during life, the moment of transformation requires either another agent's hand or — in the case of Nightwights — a framework already in motion that the candidate has been embedded in by external action. This rule has no exceptions in Thalorna's cosmology.

  

The Helspawn divide into two major classes, distinguished by intelligence, means of creation, and the integrity of the bound soul.

### The Undead Hierarchy

Helspawn exist on a spectrum defined by the completeness of the soul bound to the body. At one extreme are the [[Nightwights]] — intelligent, powerful, and semi-permanent, created through deliberate ritual from willing and powerful subjects whose souls were intercepted before entering the afterlife. At the other extreme are the [[Helthraals]] in their most degraded state — mindless, shambling corpses animated by the barest fragments of spiritual energy.

Between these poles lies the full range of undead existence. A soul pulled back from the afterlife arrives damaged, incomplete, diminished by the passage. How much of the person remains depends on how long the soul had been dead, how far it had progressed into the underworld, and the skill of whoever performed the reanimation. The recently dead, pulled back quickly by a powerful priest, may retain speech, memory, and even fragments of personality. But all Helspawn below the Nightwight tier degrade over time — the soul fragments eroding, the body decaying, identity slipping away week by week until nothing remains but the mindless hunger of a terminal Helthraal.

This degradation is universal. It is not a feature of any particular pantheon's theology but an inherent consequence of forcing a soul backward through a passage it was meant to traverse in only one direction. The gods of death, whatever their names and natures, built their underworlds as one-way systems. Reversing that flow breaks something fundamental in the soul, and the breakage cannot be repaired.

The key distinction is this: a **Nightwight** is created from a soul that *never entered the afterlife at all* — intercepted at the threshold through deliberate ritual killing and immediate reanimation. Because the soul was never damaged by the passage, it remains intact, and the resulting undead is stable and semi-permanent. A **Helthraal**, by contrast, is animated by a soul (or soul-fragment) that *has* passed into the underworld and been pulled back out. The damage from that reversal is what makes Helthraals what they are — and what ensures they will always, inevitably, degrade.
